## The Thermoregulation Mystery: Why Merino Wool Outperforms Synthetics

To understand why the **woolino sleep sack** commands such attention, one must understand the unique physiology of human infants. Babies cannot regulate their core body temperature nearly as efficiently as adults. Because their surface-area-to-mass ratio is significantly higher and their sweat glands are not fully matured, infants are vulnerable to rapid temperature fluctuations.

Overheating, in particular, is a well-documented risk factor associated with Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS). The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) strictly advises against loose blankets in the crib, recommending wearable blankets or sleep sacks instead. However, many mass-market sleep sacks are manufactured from polyester microfleece. While polyester is inexpensive, it functions like a plastic vapor barrier, trapping moisture against the skin and inducing night sweats.

Merino wool functions completely differently:

*   **Active Microclimate Management:** Merino wool fibers are naturally crimped, creating microscopic air pockets that trap ambient body heat when cold while releasing excess heat when warm.
*   **Vapor Absorption:** Merino wool can absorb up to 35% of its dry weight in moisture vapor without feeling damp or clammy to the touch. It actively wicks perspiration away from an infant's sensitive skin before evaporation causes a sudden chill.
*   **Hypoallergenic & Gentle:** Woolino uses superfine, 17.5-micron Australian merino wool, which is soft enough to sit directly against delicate baby skin without the scratching or itching historically associated with traditional sheep's wool.
*   **Natural Flame Resistance:** Unlike synthetics, merino wool does not melt or drip when exposed to heat and possesses natural flame-retardant properties, completely eliminating the need for toxic chemical flame retardants.

## Solving the TOG Rating Dilemma: The One-Sack Solution

If you have shopped for standard baby sleep sacks, you have likely encountered the confusing TOG (Thermal Overall Grade) scale. Typically, cotton and polyester sleep sacks require parents to keep multiple versions on rotation:

*   **0.5 TOG:** Lightweight for summer rooms (74°F–78°F / 23°C–26°C)
*   **1.0 TOG:** Standard for temperate rooms (68°F–73°F / 20°C–23°C)
*   **2.5 TOG:** Padded for chilly winter nights (61°F–68°F / 16°C–20°C)

This system forces caregivers to constantly check nursery thermometers, adjust base layers, and replace multiple sleep sacks every few months as seasons shift and babies grow. 

The Woolino 4 Season Ultimate Sleep Bag circumvents TOG calculations entirely. Because it features a soft organic cotton outer shell and a 100% natural merino wool lining, it naturally self-regulates across nursery temperatures ranging from **63°F to 77°F (17°C to 25°C)**. All parents need to modify is the base layer worn underneath - such as a short-sleeve bodysuit during balmy summer nights or a long-sleeve footie pajama during midwinter.

## Practical Care: Is Merino Wool Difficult to Maintain?

A widespread objection to wool products is the assumption that they are delicate, hand-wash-only items - an absolute non-starter for exhausted parents handling spit-ups and diaper blowouts. 

Fortunately, Woolino’s wool fibers undergo a specialized, eco-friendly treatment that makes them entirely machine-washable. Furthermore, merino wool contains natural lanolin, an organic wax that makes the fibers inherently antibacterial, antimicrobial, and odor-resistant. Unlike synthetic sleep sacks that develop an inescapable sour odor after two nights, the Woolino sleep sack rarely requires frequent laundering; airing it out over a nursery chair between uses is often all that is necessary for day-to-day freshness.

### Washing Best Practices
*   **Cycle:** Machine wash on a gentle or wool cycle using cold or lukewarm water.
*   **Detergent:** Use a wool-safe or pH-neutral liquid detergent (avoid bleach, fabric softeners, or harsh enzymatic cleaners that break down protein fibers).
*   **Drying:** Line dry flat whenever possible to preserve fiber elasticity. If necessary, you can tumble dry on the "no heat" or "air fluff" cycle.
*   **Quantity Tip:** While a single Woolino can suffice, having two in rotation guarantees you are never caught unprepared when an overnight diaper leak strikes mid-wash.
