If you recently received a call or text message from a number starting with the **phone prefix 702**, you are communicating with an exchange routed through Southern Nevada. Most prominently, 702 is the definitive telecommunications identifier for the city of **Las Vegas**, along with its surrounding metropolitan region in Clark County.

Understanding how telephone numbering plans work helps consumers, remote teams, and security analysts identify incoming traffic, recognize legitimate local businesses, and filter out spoofed spam calls. ## What Geographical Area Does Phone Prefix 702 Cover?

The 702 prefix is assigned to the state of Nevada within the North American Numbering Plan (NANP). While 702 historically served the entire state of Nevada from 1947 until 1998, population growth and the rapid expansion of mobile communications necessitated geographic splits and subsequent overlays.

Today, area code and exchange prefix 702 serves the following primary municipalities:

* **Las Vegas:** The central commercial district, Downtown Las Vegas, and the Strip corridor.
* **Henderson:** Nevada's second-largest city, located directly southeast of Las Vegas.
* **North Las Vegas:** The northern industrial, logistical, and suburban hub of Clark County.
* **Boulder City:** The historic community adjacent to Hoover Dam and Lake Mead.
* **Paradise, Winchester, and Spring Valley:** Major unincorporated townships comprising the greater Las Vegas Valley.

If a caller has a 702 area code, their carrier originally issued that telephone assignment within the Clark County metropolitan boundaries.

## The 725 Overlay and 10-Digit Mandatory Dialing

Because of the exponential demand for mobile devices, IoT equipment, and VoIP connections in the Las Vegas Valley, the Nevada Public Utilities Commission introduced an overlay area code: **area code 725**.

* **Effective Date:** Implemented in 2014 to prevent telephone number exhaustion in Southern Nevada.
* **Geographic Match:** Area code 725 covers the exact same geographical territory as 702.
* **10-Digit Dialing Requirement:** Because two area codes share the identical footprint, all local calls within the Las Vegas Valley require full 10-digit dialing (Area Code + 7-digit telephone number), even when dialing a neighbor across the street.

When evaluating numbers from this region, treat area codes 702 and 725 as twin identifiers for Las Vegas metropolitan carriers.

## What Time Zone Operates in Phone Prefix 702?

Phone prefix 702 operates within the **Pacific Time Zone**:

* **Standard Time:** Pacific Standard Time (PST), which is UTC-8 (observed from early November to mid-March).
* **Daylight Saving Time:** Pacific Daylight Time (PDT), which is UTC-7 (observed from mid-March to early November).

If you are scheduling business consultations, enterprise sales calls, or remote team meetings with individuals using a 702 prefix, note that Las Vegas operates on the identical clock to Los Angeles, Seattle, and San Francisco.

## How to Identify and Verify Legitimate 702 Calls

Because Las Vegas is an international travel, convention, and financial center, phone prefix 702 is commonly targeted by automated voice spoofers and phishing rings seeking to disguise outbound robocalls as domestic Nevada businesses.

### 1. Distinguish Area Code from Central Office Exchange
In standard NANP format (+1-702-NXX-XXXX):
* **702** is the Numbering Plan Area (Area Code).
* The following 3 digits (**NXX**) represent the Central Office exchange code.
* The final 4 digits (**XXXX**) identify the individual subscriber station.

Checking the Central Office exchange code allows reverse phone registries to pinpoint the specific wire center or switch (such as a switch in Henderson versus Downtown Las Vegas).

### 2. Recognize VoIP and Virtual Call Centers
Major cloud phone services like RingCentral, Twilio, Google Voice, and Zoom Phone allow companies worldwide to purchase virtual 702 numbers. A caller showing a 702 caller ID might be an overseas customer service desk or an authorized marketing contractor operating outside Nevada.

### 3. Check for STIR/SHAKEN Caller Attestation
Modern wireless carriers display checkmarks or labels like "Caller Verified" on incoming mobile calls. This uses the cryptographic STIR/SHAKEN protocol:
* **Full Attestation (A):** The carrier verified the caller is authorized to use the 702 number.
* **Partial Attestation (B):** The carrier knows the call origin, but cannot verify number ownership.
* **Gateway Attestation (C):** An international gateway or third-party switch transmitted the call with no verification (higher spam probability).
