## What Is CoCounsel and How Does It Transform Legal Workflows?

At the core of the modern Casetext experience is CoCounsel, an autonomous legal assistant built on GPT-4o class reasoning models tailored specifically for legal practice. Unlike generic conversational chatbots that may produce confident but fictitious legal citations, CoCounsel is engineered with strict retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) constraints.

Every legal proposition generated by CoCounsel is backed by direct pinpoint citations to official reporters, statutes, and administrative regulations. The primary tasks automated within the platform include:

1. **Document Review and Discovery:** CoCounsel can ingest thousands of pages of unstructured discovery documents, answering complex factual inquiries such as whether corporate executives were notified of product defects prior to specific calendar dates.
2. **Deposition Preparation:** The platform synthesizes case pleadings, witness statements, and prior testimony to generate targeted deposition outlines and identify contradictions in opposing party disclosures.
3. **Contract Analysis and Due Diligence:** Legal teams can scan hundreds of commercial leases or vendor master service agreements (MSAs) simultaneously, flagging non-standard indemnification clauses, governing law provisions, or change-of-control triggers.
4. **Legal Research Memoranda:** Attorneys can input complex legal questions in plain natural language, receiving comprehensive research drafts complete with cited judicial precedents and Shepards-style validity indicators.

## Data Confidentiality and Attorney-Client Privilege

The primary hesitation legal organizations express when adopting generative artificial intelligence involves the risk of waiving the attorney-client privilege or violating ABA Model Rule 1.6 (Confidentiality of Information). Uploading confidential client documents into consumer AI tools that retain user inputs for future model training represents an immediate ethical violation.

Casetext addresses these professional responsibility concerns through formal commercial agreements with foundational model providers. Under Casetext's security architecture, customer data and uploaded matter files are never utilized to train public machine learning models. User sessions exist in isolated memory enclaves, and all data at rest is encrypted using AES-256 standards.
